# Synapse Econs: H1 Economics content map (draft v1)

Syllabus: Singapore-Cambridge GCE A-Level **H1 Economics, syllabus 8843** (2026 exam year).
Note: the older code 8823 has been replaced by 8843. Content below follows the 8843 outline as reproduced by
[The Economics Tutor](https://www.theeconomicstutor.com/h1-economics-syllabus/) and
[Higher Nucleus](https://highernucleus.com.sg/h1-economics-syllabus-8843/). SEAB's own PDF could not be fetched from
this environment, so check against the official 8843 document before content is locked.

## 1. How H1 is examined (drives the app's structure)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Paper | One paper, **Case Studies**, 3 hours, 80 marks, 100% |
| Structure | 2 compulsory case studies, 40 marks each |
| Each case | 2–3 pages of text, tables, charts, then 7–8 part-questions |
| Mark split per case | ~16 marks data response, ~24 marks higher-order (explain, discuss, evaluate) |
| No essays | Unlike H2, there is no essay paper |

Assessment objectives (equally weighted):

- **AO1 Knowledge**: state concepts, theories, principles correctly.
- **AO2 Interpretation**: read textual, numerical, graphical data; make valid inferences within its limits.
- **AO3 Application**: apply theory to contemporary issues and policy; build coherent chains of reasoning.
- **AO4 Evaluation**: judge issues and policies, spot unstated assumptions, reach a reasoned judgement.

Roughly 40% of marks test AO1 to AO3 only; roughly 60% also test AO4. Evaluation is the biggest single lever on grades.

## 2. Content map

Each sub-topic lists: key concepts (drill items), diagrams, typical case-study asks, and what is NOT required at H1
(so the app never drills H2-only material).

### Theme 1: The Central Economic Problem

**1.1.1 Scarcity, choice and resource allocation**
- Concepts: scarcity, unlimited wants, choice, trade-off, opportunity cost, factors of production, productive efficiency, full employment, under-utilisation.
- Perspectives: consumers, producers, government.
- Diagram: **PPC** (points inside / on / outside; shifts outward and inward; movement along = opportunity cost).
- Typical asks: "Using a PPC, explain the opportunity cost of the government's decision to…"; "Explain why choice is necessary…"

**1.1.2 Decision-making of economic agents**
- Concepts: rational agent, objectives (utility / profit / social welfare), marginalist principle (MB vs MC), constraints, perspectives, intended vs unintended consequences, review of decisions.
- Typical asks: "Discuss the factors a household / firm / government should consider when deciding…"
- Not required: cost-benefit analysis as a project appraisal tool.

### Theme 2: Markets (micro)

**2.1.1 Price mechanism**
- Concepts: signalling, incentive, rationing functions; ceteris paribus.

**2.1.2 Demand and supply analysis**
- Concepts: demand / supply and non-price determinants; change in demand vs change in quantity demanded (same for supply); equilibrium; shortage and surplus; **PED, PES** (values, determinants, interpretation); consumer expenditure and producer revenue (total revenue rectangles).
- Applications: real-world markets, **including the labour market** (wage as price).
- Diagrams: D/S shifts (single and simultaneous), elastic vs inelastic comparisons, TR areas, labour market.
- Data skills: compute % change, PED/PES from table data, describe price/quantity trends, explain simultaneous shifts.
- Not required: YED, XED, consumer and producer surplus, MRP of labour.

**2.1.3 Government intervention in markets**
- Tools: indirect taxes, subsidies, maximum price, minimum price, quotas.
- Effects on equilibrium price and quantity, consumer expenditure, producer revenue, and how PED/PES change the size of the effect.
- Diagrams: tax shift, subsidy shift, price ceiling (shortage), price floor (surplus), quota.
- Not required: tax and subsidy incidence.

**2.2.1 Micro objectives: efficiency and equity**
- Concepts: allocative efficiency at MSB = MSC, social optimum vs private (market) optimum, deadweight loss, equity (fair distribution of essential goods and services); efficient need not be equitable.
- Note: inequity is a distributional issue, not a market failure.

**2.2.2 Market failure**
- Public goods: non-rivalry, non-excludability (aware of non-rejectability), free-rider, non-provision.
- Externalities: negative (MSC above MPC, MEC) and positive (MSB above MPB, MEB); over- and under-consumption/production; DWL triangle.
- Information failure: e.g. under-estimated benefits of education/healthcare, under-estimated costs of smoking.
- Diagrams: two-diagram approach, one for negative externality, one for positive.
- Not required: asymmetric information, adverse selection, moral hazard, market dominance, factor immobility.

**2.2.3 Micro policies**
- Tools: taxes, subsidies, quotas, tradeable permits, joint provision, direct provision, rules and regulations, public education.
- Evaluate each against efficiency and equity: info needed to set the right level, enforcement and admin cost, time lag, unintended consequences, effect on low-income groups.
- Singapore examples: COE quota (car market), ERP (congestion), carbon tax, sugar-levy / Nutri-Grade labels, MediSave/MediShield, public housing (HDB), Progressive Wage Model.
- Not required: "government failure" as a named concept.

### Theme 3: The National Economy (macro)

**3.1.1 AD and AS**
- Concepts: AD = C + I + G + (X − M) and determinants of each; AS and determinants (costs, productive capacity); equilibrium national output and general price level; awareness that a rise in AD has a multiplied effect on national income.
- Singapore: trade drives living standards through AD (X − M) and AS (imported inputs, FDI, capacity).
- Diagrams: AD/AS (Keynesian-shaped or upward sloping AS as taught in school), AD shift, AS shift, increase in full-employment output.
- Not required: autonomous vs induced spending, MPC/MPS, multiplier formula, circular flow.

**3.2.1 Standard of living and indicators**
- Concepts: material vs non-material standard of living; real national income per capita; income distribution, leisure, environment.
- Indicators: real GDP/GNI, real GDP/GNI per capita, unemployment rate, CPI, HDI, Gini coefficient (via Lorenz curve).
- Skills: nominal vs real, reading index numbers (base year, weights), comparing over time and across economies, limits of each indicator.
- Not required: computing index numbers, national income, HDI, or Gini.

**3.2.2 Macro issues**
- Undesirable growth: persistently low or negative, unsustainable, non-inclusive.
- Unemployment: demand-deficient, structural, frictional.
- Price instability: demand-pull inflation, cost-push inflation, deflation.
- Consequences for standard of living and for households, firms, government.
- Not required: balance of trade as a macro issue (H2 only).

**3.2.3 Macro policies**
- Fiscal policy (discretionary G and T), supply-side policies (quantity, quality, mobility of factors), monetary policy (**exchange-rate policy for Singapore**, interest rates for other economies).
- Also: different governments prioritise different objectives by state and development level; long-term fiscal sustainability; transfer payments for distribution and inclusive growth.
- Singapore examples: MAS S$NEER band, SkillsFuture, Budget support packages, GST Voucher, Workfare, foreign worker levies, Reserves / NIRC.

### Not in H1 at all (H2 only)
Firms and market structures; globalisation and international trade theory; balance of payments; circular flow; multiplier formula; YED/XED; consumer and producer surplus; advanced market failure causes; government failure; cognitive biases.

## 3. Proposed labs (Payirchi equivalents)

Tamil labs map to Paper 2 sections. H1 has one paper, so Econs labs map to **skills (AOs) across the three themes**, finishing with full case-study sets.

| Lab | Trains | Drill format | Carries over from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Concepts | AO1 | Term ↔ definition both ways, look-alike distractors (e.g. "change in demand" vs "change in quantity demanded") | Sol (vocab, both directions) |
| 2. Diagrams | AO1, AO3 | Pick the shift, predict new P and Q, shade TR / DWL, build-the-diagram step by step | Seyyul (build-the-line) |
| 3. Data response | AO2 | Read a table or chart, compute % change / PED, real vs nominal, "compare the trend" with model answers | Vetrumai (structured items) |
| 4. Chains of reasoning | AO3 | Order the steps of an analysis chain (e.g. tax → cost ↑ → S shifts left → P ↑ Q ↓ → TR depends on PED) | Echam (link the parts) |
| 5. Evaluation | AO4 | Spot the unstated assumption, pick the strongest judgement, "it depends on…" factors | Oli (contrast pairs) |
| 6. Case study sets | All | Timed 40-mark cases with mark-scheme self-marking and confidence check per part | Past paper sets in every Tamil lab |

All labs keep: retrieval first, spaced return of misses ("comes back in three cards"), confidence check ("I was sure / I guessed"), localStorage progress, reference sheet per topic.
